Case study

Media & entertainment

From template-heavy releases to same-day homepage and show-hub updates — without sacrificing brand or LCP on media-heavy pages.

Media Entertainment Migration
Anonymous case study. A composite migration narrative for this industry — useful for planning your own EDS program, not a named customer endorsement.

Overview

A culture and entertainment brand lived on a CMS where homepage takeovers, show launches, and promo swaps required developer deploys. Editorial teams missed moments; video and image-heavy templates hurt Core Web Vitals; every new campaign meant cloning templates.

Moving to EDS shifted the model: authors assemble heroes, rails, and embeds from a shared library; media loads in the delayed phase; experiments run on promotional modules without redeploying the site.

Where the program started

  • Homepage and hub refreshes queued behind engineering sprints.
  • Duplicate block implementations for similar layouts (hero v1, hero v2, hero “campaign”).
  • Autoplay video and third-party players in the critical path — poor LCP scores.
  • Promo testing required code changes or opaque CMS experiments.

Migration journey

  1. Inventory the editorial rhythm. Mapped weekly homepage changes, show hubs, and article templates to a small set of block sequences.
  2. Design the hero and rail system. Standardized hero, cards, and carousel variants with section metadata for spacing and background treatments.
  3. Defer heavy media. Moved video embeds and social players to lazy / delayed initialization in core scripts.
  4. Train authors on blocks, not pages. Editorial leads compose hubs from approved palettes; developers own block code only.
  5. Add experimentation hooks. Connected promo modules to the experimentation playbook for A/B on featured rails.
  6. Migrate show-by-show. Legacy article URLs preserved; new hubs launched on EDS first, then backfilled archives.

What landed on EDS

  • Same-day homepage and show-hub updates authored in DA.
  • Reusable hero and card rails across culture, music, and video hubs.
  • Deferred embed blocks for YouTube and social without blocking first paint.
  • Experimentation on promotional modules during major launches.

Technical highlights

Hero-first storytelling

Dual-image heroes and gradient overlays — Hero block cookbook patterns.

Editorial rails

Cards and carousels as the unit of reuse — one variant set, many hubs.

Deferred embeds

Players initialize after LCP — Embeddable EDS content.

Phased loading

Eager decoration for above-the-fold text; delayed for analytics and heavy widgets.

Target architecture

flowchart LR
  Editorial[Editorial authors] --> DA[Document Authoring]
  DA --> EDS[EDS delivery]
  EDS --> CDN[CDN]
  CDN --> Visitor[Visitors]
  EDS --> Media[Deferred embeds / players]

Critical path stays text-first; media and third parties load after the hero is painted.

Lessons from the journey

  • Variant discipline beats freedom. Fewer hero/card variants shipped faster than a dozen “almost the same” blocks.
  • LCP is an editorial constraint. Not every template gets autoplay video — policy matters as much as code.
  • Blocks match the news cycle. When marketing can swap rails without a deploy, EDS pays for itself in moments, not quarters.
  • Experiment on modules, not pages. Test promos and heroes as blocks, not whole-home redeploys.
