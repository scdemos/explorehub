Events & registration

Campaign microsites and registration marketing on Edge Delivery — inspirational storytelling, reliable forms, and traffic that spikes on race day.

Events Registration Migration
Anonymous case study. A composite migration narrative for this industry — useful for planning your own EDS program, not a named customer endorsement.

Overview

A major events program promoted marathons and experiences tied to park destinations. Marketing rotated seasonal campaigns; registration opened in waves; traffic spiked when calendars published. Legacy microsites were brittle, forms leaked secrets into page source, and authors could not preview sold-out states.

EDS became the campaign layer: heroes and story blocks in DA, forms proxied through workers, registration completion in systems built for PII — with marketing pages that survive peak load at the CDN.

Where the program started

  • One-off microsites per event — duplicated code and inconsistent branding.
  • Form endpoints and keys exposed in client-side markup.
  • Homepage takeovers required agency or dev cycles before each season.
  • No author preview for waitlist, sold-out, or members-only messaging.

Migration journey

  1. Standardize the campaign template. Hero + story sections + FAQ + form block — reused every season with new media and copy.
  2. Move forms to workers. JSON form definitions in content; submissions proxied to CRM/automation — secrets stay off the client.
  3. Author in DA year-round. Marketing updates dates, pricing copy, and imagery without redeploying front-end code.
  4. Deep-link registration. Primary conversion stays in the registration platform; EDS pages drive qualified traffic with clear CTAs.
  5. Load-test peak windows. CDN caching on static marketing shells; rate limits and bot protection on worker endpoints.
  6. Retire microsite sprawl. Consolidated paths under one EDS project; redirects from legacy campaign URLs.

What landed on EDS

  • Seasonal homepage and event landings updated by marketing in hours.
  • Worker-backed interest and newsletter forms without secrets in the browser.
  • FAQ and tabs blocks for logistics, policies, and schedules.
  • Stable performance during registration opening days.

Target architecture

flowchart LR
  Marketing[Marketing authors] --> DA[Document Authoring]
  DA --> EDS[EDS pages]
  EDS --> Visitor[Visitors]
  Visitor --> Form[Form block]
  Form --> Worker[Edge worker]
  Worker --> CRM[CRM / automation]

Storytelling on EDS; PII and registration completion in dedicated systems behind workers or deep links.

Lessons from the journey

  • Marketing ≠ registration stack. EDS should not become the system of record for participant data.
  • Forms need workers. Any secret in page source is a future incident.
  • Reuse beats microsites. One campaign pattern, many seasons — authors change media, not architecture.
  • Plan for spike day. Registration opens are load tests — cache and rate limits are part of the story.
